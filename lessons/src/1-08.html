Level 1 · Market basics · Lesson 1.08

Order types: market, limit, stop-loss, GTT, and CNC vs MIS

An order is an instruction to your broker. Choosing the right type decides the price you pay, whether you get filled at all, and how much you can lose. This is the most practical lesson in Level 1, and it ends with a simulator that tests your orders on real NSE prices.

About 60 minutesReal data: Reliance and TCS daily prices, Aug to Oct 2026
Part 1

The order types in plain words

Every order has three choices: what kind of price (order type), how long you'll hold (product type), and how long the order stays alive (validity).

Order types

TypeYou sayGoodRisk
Market"Buy now at whatever the best price is."Always fills (in a liquid stock)You don't control the price. In a fast or thin market you can pay much more than you saw.
Limit"Buy only at ₹1,165 or lower." (Sell: "only at ₹X or higher.")You control the price exactlyIt may never fill if the price doesn't come to you.
Stop-loss limit (SL)"If the price falls to ₹1,140 (trigger), send a sell limit order at ₹1,138."Limits your loss, and controls the worst fill priceIn a gap or crash the price can jump past your limit, and the order sits unfilled while you keep losing.
Stop-loss market (SL-M)"If the price falls to ₹1,140, sell at market."Gets you out once triggeredYou may be filled well below the trigger in a fast fall. (Many brokers no longer offer SL-M for options; it is still widely used for shares.)
GTT (good till triggered)"Watch the price for up to a year; when it touches ₹1,200, place my order."Perfect for a day job: works for weeks without youIt's held by your broker, not the exchange, and only places a normal order when triggered, so a fill isn't guaranteed.

A GTT OCO (one cancels other) holds two triggers at once, a target above and a stop-loss below. When one fires, the other is cancelled. Swing traders use it to protect every position while they're at work.

Product types

ProductMeaningUsed forKey rule
CNCCash and carry (delivery)Your swing trades and investmentsYou pay the full amount; shares come to your demat and you can hold as long as you like.
MISMargin intraday square-offIntraday trades on days offThe broker lends you leverage (up to 5 times for many stocks) and closes the position automatically before 3:30 pm.
NRMLNormal (for futures and options)F&O positions held overnightLearn-only in this course. You won't use it.

Validity

DAY orders expire at the end of the day if not filled (most orders). IOC (immediate or cancel) fills whatever it can instantly and cancels the rest. GTT orders last up to a year.

Trigger price vs limit price

This confuses everyone at first. The trigger is the alarm: nothing happens until the price touches it. The limit is the worst price you'll accept after the alarm goes off. For a stop-loss sell, the limit is set a little below the trigger, to give the order room to fill.

Course rule

Every trade you take, paper or real, has a stop-loss order placed at the same time as the entry. No exceptions. You'll learn where to put it in Levels 3 to 5.

Part 2

A real gap: TCS on 28 October 2026

Imagine you owned TCS from ₹2,520 and had a stop-loss with a trigger of ₹2,470. Here's what each kind of stop-loss would have done:

Your stop-lossWhat happened at 9:15 on 28 OctExit price
SL-M, trigger ₹2,470Price opened below the trigger, so it triggered at once and sold at market, near the openabout ₹2,449
SL, trigger ₹2,470, limit ₹2,465Triggered at the open, but the price was already below your ₹2,465 limit. The order waited. The high of the day was ₹2,461.30, so it never fillednot sold; still holding at ₹2,460 close, and at risk next day
No stop-lossNothing happened. You find out in the evening.still holding

SL-M cost you ₹21 a share more than you planned but got you out. The SL limit order looked safer but left you exposed. For swing trades in liquid Nifty stocks, many traders prefer SL-M or a wider gap between trigger and limit. You'll decide your own rule in Level 5.

Part 3

Worked examples

Example 1: A limit buy that fills

On 29 Oct 2026 Reliance opened at ₹1,159.00, with a low of ₹1,155.60 and a high of ₹1,182.10. You placed a limit buy at ₹1,160. Did it fill?

Answer: yes. The open (₹1,159) was already at or below your limit, so it filled at about ₹1,159, slightly better than your limit. A limit order never fills at a worse price.

Example 2: A limit buy that misses

Same day, you placed a limit buy at ₹1,150. Did it fill?

Answer: no. The lowest price was ₹1,155.60, which never reached ₹1,150. Your order expired at the end of the day (DAY validity). Being too greedy on price means missing the trade.

Example 3: MIS leverage

You have ₹20,000. A stock is ₹500 and the broker gives 5 times MIS leverage. How many shares can you buy intraday, and what happens if it falls 4%?

buying power = 20,000 × 5 = ₹1,00,000 → 200 shares fall of 4% = 200 × 500 × 0.04 = ₹4,000 loss = 20% of your own ₹20,000

Answer: 200 shares, and a 4% move loses a fifth of your money. With CNC you'd have bought 40 shares and lost ₹800. Leverage multiplies losses exactly as much as gains.

Example 4: A swing plan with GTT OCO

You bought 15 Reliance at ₹1,165 (CNC). Your plan: stop-loss ₹1,130, target ₹1,235. How do you protect it while at work?

  1. Place a GTT OCO sell for 15 shares.
  2. Lower trigger ₹1,130 (stop-loss). Upper trigger ₹1,235 (target).
  3. Whichever is touched first places the sell order; the other is cancelled.
risk = (1,165 − 1,130) × 15 = ₹525 reward = (1,235 − 1,165) × 15 = ₹1,050 → reward is 2 × risk
Part 4

Order simulator on real prices

Pick a real trading day, place an order, and see what would have happened using that day's open, high, low and close.

Daily candles can't show the order of moves inside the day, so the simulator assumes the order is live from 9:15 and that gaps happen at the open. Real fills also depend on queue position and liquidity.

Simulator task: one of each

  • A market buy on any day. Note the fill price.
  • A limit buy that fills, and one that doesn't. Write down why.
  • A stop-loss (SL) sell that triggers but doesn't fill. Hint: TCS, 28 Oct, trigger 2,470, limit 2,465.
  • An SL-M sell that triggers on a gap. Compare the fill with your trigger.
  • A limit sell above the open that fills at a high of the day.

Part 6

Practice task: start your trading journal

About 20 minutes. This matches the Level 1 goal of placing 5 paper orders.

  • Open a spreadsheet (or notebook) called "Trading journal". Columns: date, stock, side, order type, product, quantity, trigger, limit, fill price, why I placed it, what happened.
  • Log the 5 simulator orders from Part 4 as your first 5 entries.
  • If your broker has a GTT screen, open it (without placing anything) and find where to set a single trigger and an OCO.
  • Write in one line, in your own words, the difference between a trigger price and a limit price.
  • Write your stop-loss rule for now: "Every trade gets a stop-loss order placed at the same time as the entry."
