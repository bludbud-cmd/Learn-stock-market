Costs and taxes: what a trade really costs you

Every trade has small charges that add up: brokerage, STT, exchange fees, GST, stamp duty and DP charges. Then profits are taxed. Short-term traders pay these far more often than investors, so knowing them exactly is part of trading, not paperwork.

Part 1

The charges in plain words

When you buy and sell shares on NSE, these are charged on top of the share price. The rates below are for equity on NSE as listed on Zerodha's charges page in November 2026. Statutory charges (STT, stamp duty, SEBI fee, GST) are the same at every broker. Brokerage and DP charges vary.

ChargeWho gets itDelivery (CNC)Intraday (MIS)
BrokerageYour broker₹0 at many discount brokers₹20 or 0.03% per order, whichever is lower
STT (Securities Transaction Tax)Government0.1% on buy and on sell0.025% on sell only
Exchange transaction chargeNSE0.00307% each side0.00307% each side
SEBI turnover feeSEBI₹10 per crore (0.0001%)₹10 per crore
Stamp dutyState government0.015% on buy only0.003% on buy only
GSTGovernment18% on (brokerage + exchange charge + SEBI fee)
DP chargeDepository and brokerabout ₹15.34 per stock, per day you sellnone

The biggest cost on delivery trades is STT: 0.1% each way, so 0.2% of the trade value for a round trip. On a ₹10,000 trade that's ₹20 before anything else. On small trades the flat DP charge matters a lot: ₹15.34 on a ₹10,000 sale is another 0.15%.

How trading profits are taxed

Tax depends on how long you held the shares and how you traded. These are the rates for listed shares where STT was paid, as set in the July 2024 Budget and unchanged in the 2026 Budget. Check every year.

TypeWhenTax
STCG (short-term capital gains)Delivery shares sold within 12 months. All your swing trades.20% of the gain, plus 4% cess
LTCG (long-term capital gains)Delivery shares held more than 12 months12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year, plus cess. The first ₹1.25 lakh each year is tax-free.
Intraday profitBought and sold the same dayTreated as speculative business income: added to your salary and taxed at your slab rate
F&O profitFutures and options (learn-only for you)Non-speculative business income at slab rate

Losses

  • Short-term capital losses can be set off against short-term or long-term gains, and carried forward for 8 years.
  • Intraday (speculative) losses can only be set off against speculative profits, and carried forward for 4 years.
  • To carry any loss forward you must file your income tax return on time. If you trade intraday, you'll usually need ITR-3, not ITR-1.

Your broker's yearly tax P&L report splits your trades into these buckets. Download it every April.

Watch out

Charges are paid on every trade, win or lose. A trader who makes 100 small trades a month to earn ₹50 each can find that costs and taxes eat most of the profit. Fewer, better trades is the rule of this course.

Part 3

Worked examples

Example 1: The full cost of a ₹10,000 delivery round trip

Buy ₹10,000 of shares (CNC), sell later for ₹10,000. Brokerage ₹0. What do charges total?

STT 0.1% × 10,000 × 2 = ₹20.00 Exchange 0.00307% × 20,000 = ₹0.61 SEBI fee 0.0001% × 20,000 = ₹0.02 Stamp duty 0.015% × 10,000 (buy) = ₹1.50 GST 18% × (0 + 0.61 + 0.02)= ₹0.11 DP charge one sale = ₹15.34 Total ≈ ₹37.58 (0.38% of ₹10,000)

Answer: about ₹38. So the share must rise about 0.38% just for you to break even. This is the Level 1 goal calculation. You can check it in the calculator below.

Example 2: The same ₹10,000 as an intraday trade

Brokerage 0.03% × 10,000 = ₹3 per order × 2 = ₹6.00 STT 0.025% × 10,000 (sell) = ₹2.50 Exchange 0.00307% × 20,000 = ₹0.61 SEBI fee = ₹0.02 Stamp duty 0.003% × 10,000 (buy) = ₹0.30 GST 18% × (6 + 0.61 + 0.02) = ₹1.19 Total ≈ ₹10.62 (0.11%)

Answer: cheaper per trade, about ₹11, because STT and stamp duty are lower and there's no DP charge. But intraday traders trade far more often, and the profit is taxed at your slab rate, which may be 20% or 30%.

Example 3: Tax on a year of swing trading

In one year you made ₹60,000 of short-term gains and ₹18,000 of short-term losses on delivery trades. What tax do you owe on them?

net STCG = 60,000 − 18,000 = ₹42,000 tax = 20% × 42,000 = ₹8,400 cess = 4% × 8,400 = ₹336 total = ₹8,736

Answer: ₹8,736. If instead you'd held the winners over 12 months, the gains would be LTCG, and ₹42,000 is under the ₹1.25 lakh exemption: no tax at all. Long-term investing gets a much better tax deal than trading.
