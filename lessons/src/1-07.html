Market timings: pre-open, the normal session and holidays

The Indian stock market opens at 9:15 am and closes at 3:30 pm, Monday to Friday. But a lot happens before and after those times, and the first and last minutes behave differently from the rest of the day. This lesson builds the daily clock you'll trade around a 9-to-6 job.

Part 1

The trading day on NSE (equity cash market)

9:00Pre-open 9:15Normal market 3:30 3:40Post-close

Time (IST)SessionWhat happens
9:00 – 9:08Pre-open: order entryYou can place, change and cancel orders. Nothing trades yet. It closes at a random moment in the last minute, so nobody can game the final second.
9:08 – 9:12Pre-open: matchingThe exchange finds the single equilibrium price where the most shares can be matched, and trades all matched orders at that price. This becomes the stock's opening price.
9:12 – 9:15BufferA short pause to move unmatched orders into the normal market.
9:15 – 3:30Normal marketContinuous trading. Orders match instantly whenever a buyer's price meets a seller's price.
3:00 – 3:30Closing price windowThe official closing price is the volume-weighted average price of trades in these 30 minutes, not the last trade.
around 3:15 – 3:25Intraday square-offBrokers automatically close open MIS (intraday) positions. Each broker sets its own time and may charge a fee.
3:40 – 4:00Post-closeYou can buy or sell only at the official closing price.
evening to 9:00After-market orders (AMO)Your broker accepts orders and sends them to the exchange at the next open. Exact AMO hours differ by broker.

Days the market is closed

  • Every Saturday and Sunday, unless the exchange announces a special session (for example the live session on Saturday 1 February 2025, Union Budget day).
  • Trading holidays published by NSE each December for the next year: Republic Day, Holi, Good Friday, Independence Day, Gandhi Jayanti, Diwali (Laxmi Pujan), Christmas and others. About 14 to 16 weekdays a year.
  • Muhurat trading: on Diwali, NSE and BSE run a special one-hour session for the auspicious start of the new Samvat year. Timings are announced a few weeks before.

The shape of a normal day

The day has a rhythm you'll study in Level 4. The first 15 to 30 minutes after 9:15 are the most volatile: overnight news gets priced in and big gaps appear. The middle of the day (roughly 11:30 to 1:30) is usually quieter. The last hour gets busy again as funds and intraday traders close positions.

Your clock as a swing trader with a day job

  1. Evening (after 6 pm): review charts using the day's final prices. Decide on 0 to 3 trade ideas, each with an entry, a stop-loss and a target.
  2. Before 9:00 am: place an AMO or a GTT order (next lesson) with the exact entry, and a stop-loss GTT ready.
  3. During work: don't watch the screen. Your orders and stop-losses do the work.
  4. Lunch (optional): a quick check that orders behaved as expected.
Remember

A swing trader doesn't need to watch the market open. Planning in the evening and letting pre-set orders work is not a weakness: it removes the impulsive decisions that hurt most beginners.

Part 2

Two days that show why the open matters

Look at what these days mean for each kind of trader:

  • Anyone who bought at the 3 June open paid the gap: most of the move had already happened overnight.
  • An intraday trader long on 4 June without a stop-loss could lose 8% in a few hours. With MIS leverage, much more.
  • A swing trader with a stop-loss already in the system was taken out automatically, which hurts, but is exactly what a stop-loss is for. Without one, they had to decide in a panic, at work, on a phone.
  • News days that are known in advance (results, the Budget, RBI policy) are days to reduce position size or stay out.

Example 1: Finding the pre-open price

At 9:08 these orders are waiting for a stock. What opening price does the exchange choose?

Orders behind this table: buy 100 @ 1,205, 200 @ 1,200, 300 @ 1,195, 200 @ 1,190; sell 150 @ 1,190, 250 @ 1,195, 200 @ 1,200, 300 @ 1,205.

Answer: ₹1,195, because the most shares (400) can trade there. Everyone matched trades at ₹1,195, even a buyer who was willing to pay ₹1,205. That's the open price.

Example 2: Gap size

The Nifty closed at 22,530.70 on 31 May 2024 and opened at 23,337.90 on 3 June. How big was the gap?

gap = 23,337.90 − 22,530.70 = 807.20 points = 807.20 ÷ 22,530.70 = +3.58%

Answer: a 3.6% gap up, one of the biggest in years. No order placed during market hours on 31 May could have been filled anywhere in between.

Example 3: Planning around a day job

You finish work at 6 pm on Wednesday and spot a swing trade idea. When do you act?

  1. Wednesday evening: write the plan (entry ₹1,200, stop-loss ₹1,165, target ₹1,270).
  2. Wednesday night or before 9:00 Thursday: place a GTT buy order that triggers if the price reaches ₹1,200, plus a stop-loss.
  3. Thursday: go to work. If price hits ₹1,200 the order fires; if not, nothing happens.

Answer: you never needed to be at the screen at 9:15. You'll learn exactly how GTT and stop-loss orders work in lesson 1.08.

Part 5

Practice task: your trading calendar

About 15 minutes.

  • Download or open NSE's trading holiday list for this year (nseindia.com, "Holidays" under Resources). Note the next 3 holidays.
  • Check this year's Muhurat trading date and time on the NSE website.
  • On one weekday morning, open your broker's app (or nseindia.com) at about 9:10 and look at the pre-open price for Reliance or the Nifty. Compare it with the 9:15 price and the price at 9:45.
  • Write your personal weekly routine: which evenings you'll review charts, and which day off you'll use for intraday practice.
  • Find your broker's AMO time window and MIS square-off time, and write them down.
